---
title: The chatbot trap
description: Why seat activation and persona-first design stall, and what unit of progress replaces them.
---

Enterprise AI rollouts often measure success by seats activated, prompts shared, or workshop completion. Those metrics can all move while the work does not change. The trap is treating **access to a chat window** as the unit of progress.

## Three wrong units

| Unit | What leadership sees | What actually happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Seat / license** | "65% daily AI use" | People experiment; nothing ships repeatedly |
| **Persona / role** | "We built an SEO agent" | Charter grows; context rots; drafts pile up |
| **Prompt** | "We have a prompt library" | Judgment lives in someone's head; untestable |

Each wrong unit shares a problem: **no exit condition**. There is no moment where the system can say "done", only "the session ended."

## The alternative

Replace the unit with **one bounded workflow** this quarter:

- A named outcome ("weekly pulse PDF in Shared Drive")
- An exit condition ("grader passed; numbers reconcile")
- Evidence that is checked independently of the model's opinion

That workflow might use AI inside it. It is not a chatbot you visit when inspired.

## Discussion

Ask the room: **What is our default unit today, seat, persona, or workflow?**

Listen for language. "We bought Copilot" is seat thinking. "We need a research agent" is often persona thinking. "We need the weekly report to stop taking six hours" is workflow thinking.

:::note[Facilitator]
Pause here. One sentence from each person is enough. You are diagnosing the room, not debating tools.
:::
